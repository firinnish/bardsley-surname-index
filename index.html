About the data and Bardsley's abbreviations

Each row is one name as printed in a citation. People named together in one record (a married couple, a parent and child, a man and his wife, a name and its alias) each have their own row, sharing that record's date, place and source. Click a row to see the line as printed; click the page number to see the scan, with the line boxed (dashed where the position is estimated).

"Listed under" is the dictionary entry (sub nomine) the name appears in, which works in any edition; page numbers are for the 1901 edition. By default the index hides entries dated after 1650 (the SCA cut-off) and undated entries; untick the boxes to see them. Forenames that only exist inside someone else's patronymic (Adam, from "John fil. Adam") are hidden, and shown under "Possible constructed forenames based on patronyms" only when a name search finds nothing else.

Dates are as printed. Regnal years are converted for the year filter, so "20 Edw. I" counts as 1291–92. Location is the county, or London; where the book gives no place but the source is local (such as the 1379 West Riding poll tax), the location is taken from the source.
